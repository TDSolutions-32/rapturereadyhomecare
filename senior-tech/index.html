Cell phones for seniors: a buyer’s guide

Flip phone or smartphone? Which features matter, how to pick a plan and how to set it up so it’s easy to use.

September 28, 2026

The right phone is the one you’ll actually use. Before you look at brands or prices, think about what you want the phone to do for you.

Start with how you’ll use it

  • Calls and texts only. A simple flip phone may be all you need, and it usually costs less.
  • Photos and video chats with family. A smartphone makes this easy, and most can be set up with larger text and simpler screens.
  • Health and safety. Look for emergency calling, fall detection, medication reminders or a way to share your location with family.

The three kinds of phones

Flip phones

Real buttons, a long-lasting battery and very little to learn. Good for calling and texting. Many have large keys, loud speakers and an emergency button.

Simplified smartphones

Smartphones built or set up for older adults, with big icons and fewer menus. Many standard Android phones also include an “easy mode” that does the same thing.

Standard smartphones

An iPhone or Android phone does it all: video calls, telehealth visits, photos, maps and apps. If your family already uses iPhones, an iPhone can make sharing photos and video calls simpler.

Features worth checking in the store

  • Screen and text size. Hold the phone and read a message. Can you see it comfortably without your glasses?
  • Sound and hearing. Make a test call. If you use hearing aids, look for the hearing aid compatibility (HAC) rating on the box. The FCC explains the ratings here.
  • Emergency features. Most smartphones can call 911 and alert your emergency contacts with a few button presses. Some also detect falls.
  • Battery life. A phone that lasts a full day or more means fewer worries about charging.
  • Buttons and grip. Make sure the buttons are easy to press and the phone doesn’t feel slippery in your hand.

Choosing a plan

Plans can cost more than the phone over time, so it pays to match the plan to how you use it.

  • Talk and text only: the lowest-cost option if you don’t need the internet on your phone.
  • Prepaid: pay ahead each month with no contract, and no surprise bills.
  • Plans for 55 and older: several major carriers offer them. Ask exactly what’s included and whether prices change after a promotion ends.
  • Coverage at home: check each carrier’s coverage map for your own address before you sign up, and ask neighbors which service works well for them.

Bring a family member or friend along when you shop, and ask the store to transfer your contacts and photos before you leave.

Set it up to be easy to use

  • Make the text and icons larger in the phone’s display settings.
  • Turn on live captions for calls and videos if hearing is a challenge.
  • Add emergency contacts and your medical information so first responders can see it.
  • Put the people you call most on the home screen as favorites.
  • Turn on spam call filtering to cut down on unwanted calls.

Stay safe from phone scams

  • Never share a code that was texted to you with someone who calls or texts asking for it.
  • Medicare, Social Security and your bank won’t call to demand payment or gift cards.
  • If a call feels urgent or frightening, hang up and call back using a number you already know.
